## 1. Executive Summary

AIBS Digital Setup Guides is a free public knowledge website for Papua New Guinea homes, SMEs, parents, churches, schools, internet café operators, gaming zone operators, and small office users.

The website provides practical, step-by-step setup guides covering internet safety, router security, passwords, two-factor authentication, child online safety, content filtering, small office internet setup, internet café planning, gaming zone setup, cybersecurity basics, buying checklists, and downloadable templates.

The strategic position is simple:

> **Technology advice must fit PNG realities.**

This project is not a generic tech blog. It is a PNG-grounded digital trust and setup hub built around local constraints such as variable internet quality, mobile-first access, power interruptions, shared devices, second-hand equipment, informal buying channels, cyber-safety risk, and mixed levels of digital literacy.

---

## 2. Primary Objectives

1. Publish a trusted public library of PNG-specific digital setup guides.
2. Launch with 12 high-priority guides focused on safety, router security, child protection, and internet café basics.
3. Build toward a 50-guide knowledge base over 20 weeks.
4. Establish AIBS as a practical digital guidance authority for PNG SMEs and communities.
5. Create reusable checklists, templates, and lead magnets for future advisory services.
6. Prepare a future interactive guide platform after demand is proven.

## 4. Problem Statement

Many PNG households, SMEs, and community organisations use internet-connected devices without clear, practical, locally relevant setup guidance.

Common problems include:

- Weak router passwords and default configurations.
- Poor account recovery setup and lack of two-factor authentication.
- Children using unmanaged internet-connected devices.
- Shared devices in homes, offices, schools, and internet cafés.
- Unsafe downloads, fake links, scam messages, and marketplace risks.
- Internet café and gaming zone operators buying equipment without clear architecture.
- Advice copied from overseas contexts that ignores PNG power, internet, affordability, and support realities.

---

## 5. Why Now

PNG is moving deeper into digital service delivery, online business, mobile-first communication, cloud tools, online gaming, social commerce, and digital payments.

The public need is increasing for:

- Online safety education.
- SME digital literacy.
- Practical cybersecurity guidance.
- Trusted setup instructions.
- Locally adapted ICT buying and configuration checklists.

AIBS can enter this space with strong domain advantage because of practical PNG ICT delivery experience, Microsoft cloud work, CCTV/ELV architecture, governance documentation, and local implementation knowledge.

## 7. Scope

### In Scope

- Website structure and category pages.
- 12 launch guides.
- 50-guide roadmap.
- Downloadable checklists.
- PNG reality check sections.
- Source registers and assumptions registers per guide.
- Basic SEO titles and descriptions.
- Contact/request help form.
- About AIBS page.
- Future-ready structure for interactive tools.

### Out of Scope for MVP

- Custom user accounts.
- Paid courses.
- Marketplace transactions.
- Live technical support chat.
- Automated router scanning tools.
- Product sales.
- Formal legal or cybersecurity incident response advice.
- Claims that any specific supplier, ISP, product, or platform is “best” without verified evidence.

---

## 8. MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is successful when:

1. Website is live and mobile-accessible.
2. Logo, brand colours, and PNG visual identity are correctly applied.
3. 12 launch guides are published.
4. Every guide has a checklist, PNG reality check, warnings, and source/assumption register.
5. Navigation works across all categories.
6. Contact/request help form works.
7. Basic analytics are installed.
8. All high-risk claims are removed, cited, or tagged.
9. Owner has reviewed and approved the public wording.

---

## 9. Definition of Done

A guide or page is not complete until it meets the QA checklist in `QA_CHECKLIST.md`.

Minimum definition of done:

- Clear target user.
- Step-by-step instructions.
- PNG reality check.
- What to buy / what not to buy.
- Security warning section where relevant.
- Troubleshooting section.
- Printable checklist.
- Source register.
- Assumptions register.
- No unsupported factual claims.
- No placeholder text.
- Owner review completed before publication.
