How to Turn On Two-Factor Authentication for Facebook, Gmail, Microsoft, WhatsApp, and Banking Apps

Audience: Everyone
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 30 minutes
Last reviewed: June 2026

1 Quick Summary

Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second step to logging in, making it much harder for someone to access your account even if they know your password. Without 2FA, your account is only protected by one thing — your password. With 2FA, an attacker would also need physical access to your phone or app.

This guide covers how to enable 2FA on Facebook, Gmail, Microsoft accounts, WhatsApp, and banking apps — the most commonly used services in Papua New Guinea.

What you will achieve: After completing this guide, your most important accounts will require a second verification step to log in. Account takeovers — which are common in PNG, especially on Facebook — are significantly harder when 2FA is enabled.
Two types of 2FA: SMS codes (a code sent to your phone by text message) are the most common and easiest to set up. Authentication apps (like Google Authenticator or Authy) are more secure and work without mobile data or signal. This guide covers both where available.

2 Before You Start

What you need

  • Your mobile phone — you need it to receive SMS verification codes during setup
  • Access to each account you want to protect (you must be able to log in)
  • A current, working phone number registered to you — this becomes your 2FA method
  • For authentication app 2FA: install Google Authenticator or Authy from the Play Store or App Store before starting

Before enabling 2FA, make sure you have

  • A working recovery email address added to your Google and Facebook accounts
  • Your current phone number confirmed as correct in account settings
  • A secure place to store any backup codes that are generated during setup

3 PNG Reality Check

PNG Reality Check

Facebook account takeovers are a common and serious problem in PNG. Many users lose access to accounts that they use for business communication, selling goods, family contact, and sometimes mobile payments. Recovering a taken-over account without 2FA is difficult and sometimes impossible.

Enabling 2FA on Facebook, Gmail, and banking apps is one of the most effective single steps you can take to protect yourself online. Even SMS-based 2FA — the simplest form — provides significantly better protection than password-only accounts.

PNG mobile numbers using Digicel and bmobile are fully compatible with SMS-based 2FA on all major platforms. If you have a working phone number that receives SMS, you can set up 2FA today. CONFIRMED — international SMS delivery works for Digicel PNG and bmobile numbers

Note: SMS delivery can occasionally be delayed in areas with poor signal. If you use 2FA via SMS and you are in a low-signal area, you may experience delays receiving your code. An authentication app works offline once set up, making it a better option for users in areas with unreliable data connectivity.

4 Safety Warning

Never Share Your 2FA Codes

If you receive a code via SMS that you did not request — or a code arrives just as someone calls you or messages you asking for it — do NOT share it. This is a social engineering attack called "SIM swap" or "2FA bypass" fraud.

Scammers will say things like: "I'm from Facebook support and I need that code to verify your account", "BSP Bank is processing a transaction and needs the code to confirm it's you", or "There's a problem with your account — just read me the code and I'll fix it."

A real company will never ask you to read back a 2FA code over the phone or in a message. If you receive an unsolicited code, treat it as a sign that someone is attempting to access your account. Change your password immediately.

5 Step-by-Step Instructions by Platform

Facebook

Enable 2FA on Facebook

Facebook 2FA protects your account from takeovers even if your password is stolen or guessed.

  1. Open the Facebook app on your phone, or go to facebook.com in a browser.
  2. Tap the three lines menu (bottom right on iPhone, top right on Android), or click the down arrow in the top right corner on a computer.
  3. Go to Settings & Privacy → Settings.
  4. Tap Security and Login (or Password and Security on newer versions).
  5. Find Two-Factor Authentication and tap it.
  6. Tap Use Two-Factor Authentication and then tap Get Started.
  7. Choose your method: Text Message (SMS) is the easiest for most users. An authentication app is more secure — use it if you have Google Authenticator installed.
  8. Enter your phone number, receive the SMS code, and enter it to confirm.
  9. Save the backup codes that Facebook provides. Print or write them down and store safely.
After setup, Facebook will ask for a 6-digit code the next time you log in from a new device. On trusted devices you use regularly, Facebook may remember your device for 30 days.
Gmail / Google

Enable 2-Step Verification on Google

Your Google account controls Gmail, Google Drive, YouTube, and Google Pay. Protecting it with 2-step verification is critical because it is also used to recover other accounts.

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com in a browser, or tap your Google account icon in the Gmail app and tap Manage your Google Account.
  2. Tap or click the Security tab.
  3. Under "How you sign in to Google", find 2-Step Verification and tap it.
  4. Tap Get started. You may need to enter your Google password.
  5. Choose your method:
    • Google prompt — sends a notification to your phone asking "Is this you trying to sign in?" — easiest if your phone is linked to your Google account.
    • SMS or call — sends a code to your phone number.
    • Authenticator app — more secure, works without signal.
  6. Follow the on-screen steps to verify your chosen method and turn on 2-Step Verification.
  7. Once enabled, consider adding a backup option (e.g. a second phone number or backup codes).
Go to myaccount.google.com → Security → 2-Step Verification to check that it shows "On". You will also see the date it was turned on.
Microsoft

Enable Two-Step Verification on Microsoft Account

If you use Outlook email, Microsoft 365, OneDrive, Xbox, or Windows with a Microsoft account, protecting it with 2-step verification is important.

  1. Go to account.microsoft.com in a browser and sign in.
  2. Click Security in the top navigation bar.
  3. Click Advanced security options.
  4. Under "Two-step verification", click Turn on.
  5. Follow the setup wizard. Options include the Microsoft Authenticator app (recommended), email, or phone number.
  6. At the end of setup, Microsoft will give you a recovery code. Print or write this down and store it very safely. It is the only way to recover your account if you lose access to your 2FA method.
The Microsoft Authenticator app is available free from the Play Store and App Store. It works even without mobile data once set up.
WhatsApp

Enable Two-Step Verification on WhatsApp

WhatsApp's two-step verification adds a 6-digit PIN that is required when you register your phone number on a new device. This prevents someone who gets your SIM card from taking over your WhatsApp account.

  1. Open WhatsApp on your phone.
  2. Tap the three dots menu (top right on Android) or go to Settings (bottom right on iPhone).
  3. Tap Account → Two-step verification.
  4. Tap Enable.
  5. Create a 6-digit PIN. This is not a code sent to you — you create it. Choose something memorable but not obvious. Do not use your birth year or phone number.
  6. Enter the PIN a second time to confirm it.
  7. Add a recovery email address. This is used to reset the PIN if you forget it. Use a real email address you have access to — not a throwaway one.
  8. Tap Done.
WhatsApp will occasionally ask you to enter your PIN to remind you of it. If you forget the PIN and did not add a recovery email, you must wait 7 days to reset it. Write your PIN down and store it safely.
Banking Apps

Enable 2FA on Banking and Mobile Money Apps

PNG banks increasingly offer 2FA for their mobile apps. How you enable it depends on your bank.

  • BSP Mobile Banking: Contact BSP Bank or visit your nearest branch. BSP uses SMS OTP (one-time password) for transaction verification. Ensure your registered mobile number in BSP's system is current and belongs to you. REQUIRES USER CONFIRMATION — check with BSP for current 2FA options
  • Kina Bank: Check the Kina Bank app settings or call Kina Bank. REQUIRES USER CONFIRMATION — check with Kina Bank
  • Other banks and mobile money services: Check your app's Settings or Security section. Look for "Two-Factor Authentication", "2FA", "OTP", or "Transaction Verification".
For all banking apps: Ensure your bank has your current mobile number on file. SMS-based transaction alerts and OTP codes are your first line of defence against unauthorised transactions. Call your bank immediately if you notice any transactions you did not authorise.

6 After Enabling 2FA — What to Do Next

Save your backup codes

Most platforms offer backup codes when you enable 2FA. These are one-time codes you can use to log in if you lose access to your phone or 2FA method. Treat backup codes like cash — if someone else has them, they can access your account.

  • Print backup codes or write them down clearly.
  • Store them in a secure physical location — not in your phone's notes app unless that app is password-protected.
  • Do not photograph backup codes and store the photo in your camera roll — it is too easily accessible.

Add a recovery option

  • Add a recovery email to Facebook and Google accounts if you have not already.
  • Add a backup phone number where possible.
  • For Google, consider adding a "Trusted device" — a second device you own that can confirm your identity.

Check trusted devices

After enabling 2FA, review which devices are marked as "trusted" or "remembered" in your account settings. Remove any devices you no longer own or recognise.

  • Facebook: Settings → Security and Login → Where you're logged in
  • Google: myaccount.google.com → Security → Your devices

8 Troubleshooting

Problem Likely Cause What to Try
SMS code not arriving Poor signal, SMS delay, or wrong phone number entered Wait 2–3 minutes. Check signal. Request the code again. Confirm your phone number is entered correctly in the account. Try an authentication app instead.
Locked out of account after enabling 2FA Lost phone or changed phone number Use backup codes if saved. Use recovery email to reset. For Facebook, use the "Get more help" option on the login page with identity verification.
Authentication app codes not accepted Phone clock is out of sync Authentication apps use time-based codes. If your phone clock is wrong, codes will not work. Sync your phone's time: Settings → Date & Time → Automatic date & time.
Forgot WhatsApp 2-step PIN PIN not recorded Use the recovery email you set up during configuration. If no recovery email was added, you must wait 7 days for the PIN reset to process — WhatsApp will not let you register on a new device during this period.
Cannot find 2FA settings on Facebook App version or interface changed On newer Facebook versions, go to: Menu → Settings → Password and Security → Two-Factor Authentication. Settings locations change with app updates.

9 When to Get Help

  • If you are fully locked out of an account and have no backup codes or recovery options — contact the platform's official support directly (Facebook Help, Google Account support)
  • If you suspect your account has already been taken over — contact the platform immediately, and change passwords on all linked accounts
  • If your bank account shows unauthorised transactions — contact your bank immediately, not through the app
  • For business accounts or organisations needing 2FA policies across multiple staff members — consult a technical advisor
