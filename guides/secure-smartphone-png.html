Secure Smartphone Setup for PNG Users

Audience: Mobile-first users, anyone using a smartphone for banking or social media
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 30 minutes
Last reviewed: June 2026

1 Quick Summary

Your smartphone likely contains your most sensitive personal information — photos, messages, bank access, social media accounts, and personal contacts. If it is lost, stolen, or accessed by someone else, the damage can extend well beyond the phone itself.

This guide covers seven essential security steps that every smartphone owner in PNG should complete. The steps apply to both Android phones and iPhones (iOS). Where the menu path differs between platforms, both are listed.

What you will achieve: After completing this guide, your phone will have a strong screen lock, automatic backup, Find My Device enabled, app permissions reviewed, and your device protected against loss, theft, and second-hand phone risks.

2 PNG Reality Check

PNG Reality Check

Most PNG users access the internet primarily through a smartphone, not a computer. Phones are frequently shared within families, and personal and work information may mix on the same device.

Phone theft and device loss are significant risks, particularly in urban markets and public transport areas. A stolen phone without a strong screen lock gives an attacker immediate access to your Facebook, mobile banking apps, email, and photos.

Many phones in PNG are purchased second-hand. A second-hand phone may retain the previous owner's accounts, apps, or in the worst cases, spyware. Factory resetting a second-hand phone before use is essential. ASSUMED — second-hand phone market prevalence in PNG based on general market signals

Mobile data is expensive in PNG, which means automatic app updates are sometimes turned off to save data. This can leave known security vulnerabilities in apps and the operating system unpatched for extended periods. Updating on Wi-Fi where available is strongly recommended.

3 Second-Hand Phone Warning

If You Bought a Second-Hand Phone — Read This First

If you purchased a second-hand phone, always perform a factory reset before using it for your own accounts. This removes any spyware, accounts, tracking apps, or configuration from the previous owner.

Before factory resetting your own existing phone, back up your data first — photos, contacts, and messages will be deleted by a factory reset. See Step 5 in this guide for backup instructions before you reset.

If you are setting up a brand-new phone you purchased yourself, you do not need to factory reset — proceed directly to the steps below.

4 Step-by-Step: Securing Your Smartphone

Step 1

Set a Strong Screen Lock

A screen lock is the most important security control on your phone. Without one, anyone who picks up your phone has full access to everything on it.

Android: Settings > Security > Screen Lock (or Settings > Lock Screen on some models)

iOS (iPhone): Settings > Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID & Passcode on older models)

  • Use a PIN of at least 6 digits. Avoid simple sequences like 123456, 000000, or your date of birth.
  • A password (letters and numbers) is more secure than a PIN if you prefer it.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock is convenient — but always set a PIN or password as a backup, because biometrics can fail or be forced.
  • Avoid simple patterns (shape patterns on Android) — they can be guessed by watching your finger movement, known as shoulder-surfing.
After setting the lock, press the power button to lock the phone and confirm the lock screen requires your PIN or password to unlock.
Step 2

Enable Auto-Lock (Short Timeout)

Auto-lock automatically locks your phone screen after a period of inactivity. A short timeout means the phone locks quickly if you put it down or it is taken from you.

Android: Settings > Display > Screen Timeout — set to 30 seconds or 1 minute

iOS: Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock — set to 30 seconds or 1 minute

Also ensure that authentication is required immediately after locking (not after a grace period). On Android, check Settings > Security > Screen Lock > Lock Immediately. On iOS, the default is to require unlock immediately after the screen turns off.

Lock the phone and wait 30 seconds. It should require your PIN or biometric to unlock. If it unlocks without any authentication, adjust the grace period or timeout settings.
Step 3

Update Your Operating System and Apps

Operating system updates often include security patches that fix known vulnerabilities. Keeping your phone updated is one of the most effective things you can do to reduce security risk.

Android: Settings > System > Software Update (or Settings > About Phone > Software Update on some models)

iOS: Settings > General > Software Update

For app updates: open the Google Play Store (Android) or App Store (iOS) and check for pending updates. Update all apps where possible.

If your mobile data budget is limited, connect to Wi-Fi before updating. Enable automatic updates on Wi-Fi only to keep apps current without using your mobile data allowance:

  • Android: Play Store > Profile > Settings > Network Preferences > Auto-update apps > Over Wi-Fi only
  • iOS: Settings > App Store > Automatic Downloads > use Wi-Fi only
After checking for updates, the system should show "Your software is up to date" or display available updates. Install all available updates.
Step 4

Review App Permissions

Apps often request access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts, and storage. Many apps request these permissions without needing them for their core function. Reviewing and revoking unnecessary permissions reduces what apps can access without your knowledge.

Android: Settings > Apps > select any app > Permissions — or Settings > Privacy > Permission Manager to see all apps with a specific permission

iOS: Settings > Privacy & Security > select the permission type (Camera, Microphone, Location, Contacts, etc.) to see which apps have access

Questions to ask for each permission:

  • Does this app genuinely need camera access? (A flashlight app does not need your camera to operate.)
  • Does this app need to know my location at all times, or only when I am using it?
  • Does this game or social media app need access to my contacts?

Change location permissions for apps that do not genuinely need your location from "Always" to "Only while using" or "Never".

After reviewing permissions, each app should only have access to the features it genuinely needs. Revoke any permissions that seem excessive for what the app does.
Step 5

Set Up Phone Backup

A backup protects your contacts, photos, messages, and app data if your phone is lost, stolen, or damaged. Without a backup, that data is gone permanently.

Android (Google backup): Settings > Google > Backup > Back up to Google Drive — turn on and tap Back Up Now. This backs up app data, call history, contacts, and device settings. Photos need to be backed up separately via Google Photos.

iOS (iCloud backup): Settings > [Your Name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup > turn on and tap Back Up Now. iCloud backs up most app data, photos, messages, and device settings.

Set backups to run automatically over Wi-Fi so you always have a recent backup without manual effort or mobile data use.

After enabling backup, confirm the last backup date shows as "Just now" or today's date. This confirms backup is working correctly.
Step 6

Enable Find My Device

Find My Device (Android) or Find My iPhone (iOS) allows you to locate, lock, or remotely erase your phone if it is lost or stolen. This is only useful if it is turned on before the phone is lost.

Android: Settings > Security > Find My Device — ensure it is turned on. You can use it later at myaccount.google.com/find-your-phone from any browser.

iOS: Settings > [Your Name] > Find My > Find My iPhone — turn on. Also enable "Send Last Location" so the phone reports its location before the battery dies. Use at icloud.com/find from any browser.

You must be signed into your Google account (Android) or Apple ID (iOS) for Find My Device to work. Make sure you remember the email address and password for that account.

After enabling, log into the relevant web portal from a computer and confirm your device appears in the list. This confirms Find My Device is active and working.
Step 7

Check for Suspicious or Unnecessary Apps

Unfamiliar or unused apps may be consuming resources, collecting data, or presenting security risks. This step is especially important for second-hand phones.

  • Open your app list and look for apps you do not recognise or have not used in months. Uninstall them.
  • Check Settings > Apps for apps that are installed but not visible on the home screen.
  • Never install apps from sources outside the Google Play Store (Android) or App Store (iOS). Apps from unknown sources — downloaded as APK files or from unofficial websites — have not been reviewed by Google or Apple and carry a significant risk of malware. ASSUMED — sideloading of unverified apps is a risk pattern; confirm prevalence in PNG with local data
  • If you are on Android, go to Settings > Apps > Special App Access > Install Unknown Apps and ensure no apps have permission to install other apps — unless you specifically need this for a trusted application.
Your app list should only contain apps you installed yourself or manufacturer pre-installed apps. Any app you cannot identify should be researched before keeping it.

5 Recommended Settings Summary

Setting Recommended Value Why
Screen lock 6-digit PIN or strong password Prevents access to phone contents if lost or stolen
Auto-lock timeout 30 seconds to 1 minute Minimises the window of opportunity if you put the phone down
Install from unknown sources Disabled Prevents malware-laden apps from being installed outside official app stores
Automatic app updates Over Wi-Fi only Keeps apps updated with security patches without using mobile data
Backup frequency Daily, automatic over Wi-Fi Protects data against loss, theft, or device damage
Find My Device / Find My iPhone Enabled Allows remote locate, lock, or wipe if the phone is stolen
Location permissions for most apps Only while using the app Reduces constant location tracking by apps that do not need it
