How to Set Up Content Filtering for a Small Office

Audience: SME owners, school administrators, church IT helpers, office managers
Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 45 minutes
Last reviewed: June 2026

1 Quick Summary

Content filtering in a small office protects staff from accidentally downloading malware, reduces access to inappropriate content, and helps enforce a basic internet usage policy. This guide covers DNS-based filtering at the router level, which applies to all devices on the network without needing software installed on each computer or phone.

A single change at the router level affects every device on the office network — computers, phones, tablets, and printers. This makes DNS filtering one of the most practical and cost-effective controls available for a small office in PNG.

What you will achieve: After completing this guide, your office network will have DNS-based content filtering active, you will have documented your settings, and staff will be aware of the basic internet usage expectations.

2 PNG Reality Check

PNG Reality Check

In PNG small offices, schools, and churches, internet access is often shared through a single router or Wi-Fi hotspot. Staff may use personal phones on the office Wi-Fi. Students or congregation members may access the internet through shared devices or the office Wi-Fi network.

A single DNS configuration at the router level can apply basic filtering to all devices on the network — without software on each device. This is especially practical in environments where devices change frequently, staff are not technical, or you do not have the time to configure each device individually.

Personal mobile phones connected to the office Wi-Fi will also use the office DNS filtering while connected — but will revert to unfiltered access when staff switch to their mobile data. DNS filtering covers what goes through your network, not what goes through the mobile carrier.

3 Filtering Is a Tool, Not a Policy

Content filtering must be backed by a clear policy. Filtering is a technical control. It works best when staff understand what internet use is acceptable, why restrictions are in place, and what the consequences of misuse are. A DNS filter applied silently without staff awareness can create confusion and resentment. Even a simple one-page internet usage policy is more effective than filtering alone.

A written internet usage policy for your office should cover at minimum:

  • What internet use is acceptable during work hours
  • What is prohibited (illegal downloads, adult content, excessive personal use)
  • That the office network is monitored and filtering is in place
  • Consequences for misuse

A full internet usage policy guide is planned for Guide 38 in this series. For now, even a short document signed by staff is more effective than no policy at all.

4 Step 1 — Define What You Want to Filter

Before changing any settings, decide what categories of content you want to block. This avoids over-blocking (reducing productivity) or under-blocking (leaving staff and the network exposed to risk).

Category Recommendation Notes
Malware and phishing sites Block — strongly recommended for all offices Protects against accidental malware downloads and credential theft. No productivity impact.
Adult content Block — recommended for public-facing spaces, schools, churches Essential where minors may use the network or where professional reputation matters. Minimal productivity impact in a professional office.
Social media Consider allowing during breaks, not blocking outright Blanket blocking of social media can create friction and resentment. Time-based restrictions are more proportionate if overuse is a concern. ASSUMED — confirm with PNG SME management practice
Gaming and streaming sites Consider restricting during work hours High-bandwidth sites can affect connection speed for work tasks. Work-hours-only restriction is a reasonable middle ground.

5 Step 2 — Choose a DNS Filtering Service

The following free DNS services are suitable for small office use in PNG. All are publicly available without account registration for basic use:

Service Primary DNS Secondary DNS What It Blocks Best For
Cloudflare (standard) 1.1.1.1 1.0.0.1 Malware and phishing only General office — maximum speed, minimal restrictions
Cloudflare Family 1.1.1.3 1.0.0.3 Malware, phishing, and adult content Schools, churches, offices serving minors
OpenDNS Basic (free tier) 208.67.222.222 208.67.220.220 Customizable via OpenDNS account Offices wanting more control over categories — requires account setup
OpenDNS FamilyShield 208.67.222.123 208.67.220.123 Adult content (fixed category) Schools and community spaces — no account needed

Recommended for most PNG small offices: Use Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 / 1.0.0.1 for a general business office (malware and phishing protection with no content restrictions). Use Cloudflare Family 1.1.1.3 / 1.0.0.3 for schools, churches, or offices where staff interact with minors or where adult content would be professionally inappropriate.

6 Step 3 — Apply DNS Filtering to Your Router

The process for entering DNS settings into your router is the same as for home routers. If you have not done this before, review the Secure Your Wi-Fi Router guide first for instructions on accessing your router admin panel.

Step 3a

Access the Router Admin Panel

Open a browser on a computer or phone connected to the office network. Go to 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 (or the address shown on your router's label). Log in with the admin credentials.

You should see the router's admin dashboard after logging in.
Step 3b

Find WAN or DNS Settings

Navigate to the WAN, Internet, or DNS settings section. Look for fields labelled "Primary DNS Server" and "Secondary DNS Server". Write down the existing values before changing them.

Two DNS fields should be visible. They may currently show your ISP's DNS addresses.
Step 3c

Enter Your Chosen DNS Addresses

Enter the Primary DNS and Secondary DNS addresses for the service you chose in Step 2. Double-check each digit — DNS addresses must be entered exactly. Click Save or Apply.

After saving, the fields should show your new DNS addresses when you reload the settings page.
Step 3d

Restart the Router

Restart the router to ensure all connected devices pick up the new DNS settings. Use the router admin's restart option or power cycle the router (off for 10 seconds, then back on). Wait for the network to come back up (usually 30–60 seconds).

All devices should reconnect automatically. Internet access should resume as normal.

7 Step 4 — Test That Filtering Is Working

Test 1

Verify DNS Is Active

From a browser on a device connected to the office Wi-Fi, go to https://1.1.1.1/help. This Cloudflare page shows which DNS server your device is currently using. If you set Cloudflare DNS, it should confirm the connection is using Cloudflare.

The page should confirm your DNS is going through Cloudflare's servers.
Test 2

Test Malware Blocking

If you are using Cloudflare (malware blocking), visit https://malware.testcategory.com — this is a safe test domain maintained by Cloudflare to confirm malware category blocking is working. It should fail to load or show a Cloudflare block page if filtering is active.

The test domain should be blocked and not load in the browser.
Test 3

Confirm Normal Business Sites Work

Verify that your normal work websites — email, government services, supplier sites, banking — all continue to load correctly. If any legitimate business sites are being blocked, the DNS service may be too aggressive. Switch to Cloudflare standard (1.1.1.1) if Cloudflare Family is over-blocking.

All legitimate business sites should load without issues.

8 Step 5 — Document Your Settings

Documenting your network settings is an important and frequently skipped step. When a staff member needs to troubleshoot connectivity or when the person who configured the network is unavailable, having a written record prevents hours of confusion.

Record the following and store it in a secure location (a locked file, the business owner's records, or a trusted administrator's notes):

Item to Record Example
DNS service chosen Cloudflare Family (1.1.1.3 / 1.0.0.3)
Date of setup June 2026
Who configured the settings Name and contact number
Router admin IP address 192.168.1.1
Router admin username admin (or custom username)
Router admin password storage location Sealed envelope in owner's file, not written in this document
Original ISP DNS addresses (in case you need to revert) Written down before changing

9 Common Mistakes

Mistake Why It Matters Fix
Blocking too aggressively and affecting productivity Overly strict DNS filtering can block legitimate business sites and tools, frustrating staff and reducing output Start with Cloudflare standard (malware only) and move to more restrictive settings only if there is a specific reason — review blocked sites monthly
Not documenting settings When the person who set up filtering leaves or is unavailable, no one knows what DNS is in use, why certain sites are blocked, or how to adjust the settings Complete the documentation table in Step 5 and store it with the office's ICT records
Not having a policy to back up the technical controls Filtering without an internet usage policy leaves staff without clear guidance. It can also create legal ambiguity if staff misuse is ever disputed Create and communicate a simple internet usage policy, even a one-page document, before or shortly after enabling filtering
Forgetting that personal mobile data bypasses the filter Staff using their own mobile data on personal phones are not subject to the office router's DNS settings Acknowledge this limitation in the usage policy. Network filtering applies to the office Wi-Fi only.
