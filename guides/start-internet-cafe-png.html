How to Start a Small Internet Café in PNG: Beginner Roadmap

Audience: Entrepreneurs, youth business owners, community ICT helpers
Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 60 minutes reading
Last reviewed: June 2026

1 Quick Summary

Starting an internet café in PNG requires planning your location, estimating startup costs, choosing a suitable ISP, setting up your hardware and network, configuring safety controls, and establishing a pricing model. This guide provides a realistic beginner roadmap — not a guarantee of success, and not a replacement for local business research and advice.

The café model that works in Port Moresby may not work the same way in Lae, Goroka, or a provincial town. Location, local demand, competition, and the range of services you offer will all shape whether a café becomes viable. Use this guide as a planning framework and check every financial estimate against current local prices before committing money.

This guide covers planning, not accounting. All cost estimates in this guide are indicative planning figures only. They are marked where confirmation is needed and should not be used as a budget without verification from current PNG hardware suppliers and ISPs.

2 PNG Reality Check

PNG Reality Check

Internet cafés in PNG face a specific set of challenges that differ from other countries. Variable internet connectivity means your service quality depends heavily on which ISP you choose and what your location can support. Power interruptions in many areas require investment in backup power such as UPS units and a generator, adding to startup and running costs.

Competition from mobile internet has reduced demand for basic web browsing, but has not eliminated the café model. Services that customers cannot easily do on a phone — printing, scanning, typing a formal letter, filling out government forms, accessing government portals — continue to drive café use in areas near government offices, markets, and schools. ASSUMED — confirm with PNG café operator survey

Hardware theft is a risk in some areas. Physical security — lockable premises, window bars or grilles, and CCTV where budget allows — should be factored into your planning from the start, not added later.

Success in the PNG café market depends on choosing the right location, understanding what your specific community needs, and managing costs carefully in the first year.

3 Before You Invest

Do Your Research Before Spending Money

Do not invest significant capital in an internet café without first visiting similar businesses in your area to understand local demand, current pricing, and what services customers are actually paying for. A site visit to 3–5 existing cafés in comparable locations will give you more useful information than any online guide.

Speak with a business development officer, AIBS advisor, or a Small Business Corporation representative before committing to large equipment purchases. ASSUMED — business advisory services available in PNG; confirm locally with Small Business Corporation PNG and AIBS

All cost estimates in this guide are planning estimates only and require confirmation with current PNG suppliers and ISPs before use in a real budget.

4 Phase 1 — Planning Your Café

Location Selection

Location is the single most important decision in starting a café. The right location means customers are nearby, willing to pay, and not already well-served by an existing café. Poor location is the most common reason cafés fail in their first year.

Location Factor Good Sign Red Flag
Proximity to schools or university Students need to print assignments, research, and access online resources School already has a well-equipped computer lab accessible to students after hours
Proximity to government offices Workers and public need to access government portals, print, and scan documents Government office has its own public computer access facility
Market or commercial area High foot traffic, traders needing business services Multiple competing cafés already operating with established customer bases
Power supply reliability Consistent grid power or easy generator setup, manageable fuel costs Frequent outages without affordable generator backup — high running costs
Physical security Lockable premises, enclosed location, community familiarity Known high-theft area without budget for security — high insurance or hardware replacement risk

Viability Questions to Answer Before Starting

  • How many internet cafés already operate within 500 metres of your proposed location?
  • What are they charging per minute or per hour?
  • What services are they offering — browsing only, or printing, scanning, and typing services?
  • What do customers there most commonly come in to do?
  • Is there demand that is currently unmet — e.g. no printer within walking distance?
  • How much does an ISP business connection cost at your address, and what speeds are available?
  • What is the cost of generator fuel per week in your area, and how many hours per day is grid power unavailable?

Visit existing cafés in person. Observe how busy they are at different times of day. Talk to customers and staff if possible. This primary research is more valuable than any secondary source.

5 Phase 2 — Startup Cost Estimates

The following estimates are indicative planning figures only. All prices are marked as requiring confirmation with current PNG suppliers. Do not use these figures as a real budget without verifying current prices.

Setup Size Computers Estimated Total Range Includes
Budget setup 3 computers K8,000 – K15,000 REQUIRES CONFIRMATION Computers, basic router, UPS units, basic printer, tables and chairs, ISP connection deposit
Standard setup 5 computers K20,000 – K35,000 REQUIRES CONFIRMATION Computers, business router, switch, UPS units, laser printer, tables and chairs, ISP connection, basic security
Standard-plus setup 8–10 computers K40,000 – K70,000 REQUIRES CONFIRMATION All above plus small generator, scanner, CCTV, improved furniture

Beyond hardware, plan for these ongoing monthly costs:

  • ISP monthly connection fee (see Phase 3)
  • Generator fuel (if power is unreliable)
  • Staff wages (if you employ someone)
  • Rent
  • Printer ink or toner
  • Electricity
Rule of thumb: Your monthly income needs to exceed all your monthly costs before you profit. Calculate your break-even point — the number of customer hours per day you need to cover costs — before opening. This is the most important financial calculation to do before spending any money.

6 Phase 3 — Choosing Your ISP

Your internet connection quality will define customer satisfaction more than any other single factor. A café with slow or unreliable internet will lose customers quickly regardless of how good the hardware is.

Questions to Ask Each ISP

  • What is the monthly cost for a business connection at this address?
  • What is the maximum download speed available at this location?
  • What is the CIR (Committed Information Rate) — the minimum speed guaranteed even at peak times?
  • Is there a data cap per month, or is it truly unlimited?
  • What is the installation fee and how long does installation take?
  • What is your uptime SLA (service level agreement) — what happens if the connection goes down?
  • Is a static IP address available and what does it cost?

PNG ISP options to contact for business connections REQUIRES USER CONFIRMATION — current PNG ISP availability and pricing:

  • Digicel Business PNG

Get written quotes from at least two ISPs before deciding. Ask about installation timeframes — some business connections in PNG can take 4–8 weeks to install. ASSUMED — confirm current installation timelines with ISPs

For a café serving 5 computers simultaneously: Aim for a minimum of 20 Mbps download speed with a reasonable CIR. More computers or gaming services require proportionally more bandwidth. Confirm with the ISP what speeds are realistically achievable at your specific address.

7 Phase 4 — Basic Hardware Setup

Detailed hardware specifications for Budget, Standard, and Premium café setups are covered in the companion guide: Internet Café Hardware Requirements. This section provides the minimum planning overview.

Minimum Requirements Per Customer Computer

  • Processor: Intel Core i3 10th generation or AMD Ryzen 3 (minimum for Windows 11 with acceptable performance)
  • RAM: 8GB (minimum — 16GB preferred for smoother operation)
  • Storage: 256GB SSD (faster loading than a traditional hard drive)
  • Monitor: 19–21 inch LED, 1080p resolution preferred
  • Operating system: Windows 11 (check hardware requirements before purchasing)

Basic Network Layout

The basic network for a small café follows this path:

ISP Modem/ONT → Business Router → Network Switch → Customer Computers + Printer

Use ethernet cable (Cat5e or Cat6) from the switch to each computer for reliable, fast connections. Wi-Fi is acceptable for a small number of customer laptops or phones but wired connections are more stable for desktop computers.

Separate staff and customer networks: If your router supports VLANs or a guest network, create a separate network for staff use (billing, administration) from the customer browsing network. This prevents customers from accessing your administrative systems.

8 Phase 5 — Safety Controls and Policies

Operating a public internet café involves responsibilities that go beyond providing connectivity. You are responsible for the safety of the environment and for putting reasonable controls in place to prevent misuse.

Customer Registration

Consider keeping a register of customers — name and a form of ID at the time of use. This creates accountability and may be required by local regulation. REQUIRES USER CONFIRMATION — check with PNG ICCC, local government, or police on any customer registration obligations for internet café operators

Acceptable Use Policy — Post This on Your Wall

At minimum, post a simple notice visible to all customers that states:

  • No illegal content, illegal downloads, or piracy
  • No accessing or distributing adult content
  • No threatening, harassing, or defaming others online
  • Time limits apply — state your specific time allocation
  • Staff may monitor screen activity at any time
  • Violations result in immediate removal without refund

Content Filtering

Apply DNS-based content filtering to your router to block adult content and malware. See the Content Filtering for Small Offices guide for step-by-step instructions. For a public café, Cloudflare Family (1.1.1.3) is recommended as it blocks both adult content and malware sites without significantly affecting performance.

Daily Operations — Computer Hygiene

  • At the end of each day, or between sessions if possible: clear browser history on all computers, log out of any accounts that may have been left open, restart each computer to clear session data
  • Run a malware scan weekly on all customer computers
  • Keep Windows and browsers updated — schedule updates during closed hours
  • Consider using Deep Freeze or a similar session reset tool that restores each computer to a clean state on restart (reduces maintenance significantly in a high-use café) ASSUMED — Deep Freeze and equivalents are available and used in PNG cafés; confirm local availability

9 Phase 6 — Pricing Your Services

Pricing needs to cover your costs, be competitive with nearby cafés, and reflect the value you provide. Start by finding out what other cafés in your area charge, then calculate whether that rate covers your costs.

Common Pricing Models

Model Example Best For
Per minute K0.30 – K1.00 per minute REQUIRES CONFIRMATION — verify current PNG market rates Short visits, checking email, quick lookups
Per hour K10 – K25 per hour REQUIRES CONFIRMATION Longer work sessions, assignment writing, job applications
Bundle packages 1 hour + 5 prints = K15 REQUIRES CONFIRMATION Customers doing document work — print, scan, browse in one session
Service pricing Print per page, scan per page, document preparation fee High-value services that justify a separate charge beyond browsing time

Services That Justify a Premium

  • Document preparation (typing a letter, CV, government form)
  • Printing — charge per page, not just per session
  • Scanning to email or USB
  • Government form submission assistance
  • Photo printing (if you have a photo printer)

Diversifying beyond basic browsing into document services significantly improves profitability per customer visit and differentiates your café from a simple internet access point.
